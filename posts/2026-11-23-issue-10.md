Several items this week share one idea: instead of hoping a learned model behaves, put the property you need into its
structure. Energy conservation, passivity, a PDE-shaped architecture, and a matching training objective each show up
below. This is a follow-up to the rollout-drift discussion in [issue 1](issue-01.html).

## Concept of the week: structure turns error growth from geometric into linear

Suppose a learned model advances a state $x_t$ by one step, $x_{t+1} = M x_t$, and you worry about how a small
one-step error $\varepsilon$ builds up. If the map amplifies perturbations by a factor $\gamma$ per step, the error after
$t$ steps behaves roughly like

$$e_t \;\approx\; \varepsilon\,\frac{\gamma^{t}-1}{\gamma-1}\quad(\gamma>1),\qquad\qquad e_t \;\le\; t\,\varepsilon\quad(\gamma\le 1).$$

When $\gamma>1$ the error grows **geometrically**: it doubles every fixed number of steps. When the map is
*non-expansive* in some norm ($\gamma\le1$), each step can add at most $\varepsilon$ and nothing is amplified, so the error
grows at most **linearly**. A way to guarantee $\gamma\le1$ is to force the model to respect an energy-like quantity: a
dissipative or energy-conserving system cannot amplify perturbations in the energy norm.

The catch, which one of this week's papers states openly (item 2), is that a constraint also restricts what the model can
represent. If the constraint does not match the physics, you buy stability at the price of bias.

## 1. Energy-preserving Bayesian model reduction

**Paper:** *Energy-preserving Bayesian model reduction: closed-form structured posteriors and probabilistic stability certificates for quadratic operator inference*. João Victor Galvão da Mata and Mengwu Guo, arXiv:2611.27794, 20 Nov 2026.[^ebmr]

**The idea in plain words.** A reduced-order model (ROM) is a small set of equations that mimics a large simulation.
*Operator inference* learns those small equations from data. The Bayesian version returns not one model but a Gaussian
distribution over the model's operators. The problem, per the authors, is that this posterior is unconstrained, so it
gives positive probability to models with unbounded dynamics; practice has been to test a few samples by simulating for a
finite time.[^ebmr]

**What's technically new.**

- Energy preservation of the quadratic operator amounts to a set of **linear equality constraints**. They can be imposed
  inside the Bayesian formulation exactly, and the result is still closed-form: the constrained posterior is one
  Kalman-type update of the unconstrained one, and samples are obtained by applying the same correction to unconstrained draws.
- Global boundedness for all initial conditions and bounded inputs reduces to a **linear matrix condition** on the symmetric
  part of the linear operator. That turns the simulation test into a **probabilistic stability certificate**: the posterior
  probability that the ROM is globally bounded, estimated with exact confidence bounds.
- For energy-conserving systems, the linear part can also be constrained to be skew-symmetric. Every draw then grows at most
  linearly in time and conserves energy exactly when unforced.

**Result.** On a forced viscous Burgers' equation, the Korteweg-de Vries equation and a two-dimensional viscous Burgers'
equation, every sample of the constrained posterior integrates to a bounded trajectory, "where up to all unconstrained
draws diverge", and beyond the observation window the constrained posterior is also the more accurate of the two.[^ebmr]
The abstract gives no further numbers.

> **Why it matters:** It makes "will this uncertain ROM blow up?" a computable probability rather than a few test runs. That
> is a useful answer to give before putting a reduced model inside a control loop.

## 2. Structure-preserving EDMD: Koopman matrices that cannot create energy

**Paper:** *Convex Structure-Preserving EDMD: Passive Koopman Matrices as Q-Contractions*. Matthias Ehrhardt and Achraf Zinihi, arXiv:2611.27730, 20 Nov 2026.[^pedmd]

**The idea in plain words.** Extended dynamic mode decomposition (EDMD) fits a matrix that advances a chosen set of
observables of the state; it approximates the Koopman operator. Plain least squares knows nothing about physics. For a
port-Hamiltonian system (one with an energy function and well-defined energy exchange with its surroundings), the
least-squares matrix is in general neither *passive* (it can create energy) nor does it preserve the system's invariants.[^pedmd]

**What's technically new.**

- The implicit midpoint discretisation of a lifted port-Hamiltonian model preserves energy balance, passivity and all linear
  Casimirs (conserved quantities) **for every step size**.
- For a fixed storage metric $Q$, the resulting Koopman matrices are Cayley transforms of $(J-R)Q$, with $J$ skew-symmetric
  and $R$ positive semidefinite. That set is the closure of all $Q$-contractions and is representable as linear matrix
  inequalities, so the constrained fit is a **semidefinite program** (convex).
- The constrained estimate is a projection of the unconstrained one and cannot increase estimation error when the true
  target is compatible with the structure.

**Result.** For every $Q$-contraction, a certified bound on the lifted prediction error grows at most linearly in time,
whereas it grows geometrically for unconstrained models with $Q$-gain above one. On a damped pendulum and a damped rigid
body, constrained models never generate energy and imposed Casimirs hold to about $10^{-8}$, while EDMD "may diverge or
lose the Casimir with noisy data".[^pedmd]

> **Why it matters, and the caveat:** This is the concept above in a clean form. The authors also note that when $Q$ is the
> physical energy, the constrained models are *less accurate* than EDMD and can show significant structural bias.[^pedmd]
> A guarantee is not free.

## 3. PSTO: learn transport once, add the chemistry later

**Paper:** *PSTO: A PDE-structured transport operator with shared implicit updates for multiscale CDR systems*. Kaiwen Li, Dezhi Zhou, Kun Wu and Xingcai Lu, arXiv:2611.25607 (v2, 20 Nov 2026; first submitted 19 Nov).[^psto]

**The idea in plain words.** Reacting flows couple spatial transport (convection and diffusion) with local, stiff
reaction kinetics, which makes them expensive to simulate. Neural surrogates usually need costly coupled
convection-diffusion-reaction (CDR) training data, and their size grows with the number of transported species. PSTO
learns the convection-diffusion part **only from reaction-free data**, and the PDE structure built into the network lets it
extend to full CDR systems at inference without retraining.[^psto]

**What's technically new.** One transport block is **shared across all transported variables**, so parameter count does not
grow with the number of species. It is refined by relaxed fixed-point iterations, an implicit-like correction in latent space
that the authors say improves stability in recursive multi-step prediction without adding trainable parameters.[^psto]

**Result.** On three benchmarks (a canonical 2D CDR system, a multiscale Robertson counterflow flame and a multiscale
hydrogen planar jet flame), PSTO reduces mean relative $L_2$ error after 100 coupled steps by **at least 50%** versus the
strongest neural baseline, and keeps its accuracy with a tenfold larger time step while baseline accuracy decays rapidly.[^psto]

> **Why it matters:** Reaction-free data is much cheaper to generate than coupled reacting-flow data. If the separation holds
> beyond these benchmarks, it is a route to surrogates for combustion without a library of reacting simulations.

## 4. Do trained DeepONet trunk bases make better preconditioners?

**Paper:** *Do DeepONet Trunk-Basis Preconditioners Need Training?* Jaemin Oh, Jerome Darbon and George Em Karniadakis, arXiv:2611.27582, 20 Nov 2026.[^tb]

**Background.** A *preconditioner* speeds up an iterative linear solver such as conjugate gradients. A two-level Schwarz
preconditioner has a *coarse space* that captures smooth, low-frequency error while a smoother removes the rest. Trunk-basis
(TB) preconditioners build that coarse space from the trunk functions of a trained DeepONet. Their effectiveness had been
attributed to *spectral bias* (networks learn low frequencies first).[^tb]

**The question and the answer.** Spectral bias comes from training, but a randomly initialised trunk with a smooth
activation is already smooth. So do TB preconditioners need training?

- In one dimension the authors prove that on each subdomain the span of untrained trunk functions is close to the space of
  low-degree polynomials, under suitable nondegeneracy assumptions.
- Numerically, for Poisson and 3D linear elasticity with constant coefficients, untrained TB and piecewise polynomial coarse
  spaces of equal dimension give **close** conjugate gradient iteration counts, and training with the standard $\ell^2$ loss
  sometimes increases them.
- Training helps when high-contrast coefficients make the low-energy modes non-smooth **and** the loss measures error in the
  energy norm. For Poisson with contrast $10^5$, energy-trained coarse spaces need fewer iterations than piecewise polynomials
  of equal dimension, whereas $\ell^2$-trained ones do not.[^tb]

> **Why it matters:** The authors' own summary is that for neural-operator-based hybrid solvers, "a network's inductive bias
> and training objective should match the solver".[^tb] It is also a good baseline discipline: compare against the untrained
> network and a polynomial space before crediting training.

## 5. Adapting an AI weather foundation model to a changing climate

**Paper:** *Adapting AI Weather Foundation Models to a Changing Climate*. Xinlei Xiong, Wenbo Hu, Shuxun Zhou, Lingxi Xie, Richang Hong and Qi Tian, arXiv:2611.27605, 20 Nov 2026.[^aurora]

**The idea in plain words.** AI weather models learn from historical archives, but the climate they are deployed into keeps
shifting. The authors want to adapt continually **without retraining the whole model**. They split forecast errors into two
kinds: geographically persistent biases, and flow-dependent errors that vary with the atmospheric state.[^aurora]

**What's technically new.** An analysis of Aurora-1.3B shows forecast-loss sensitivity concentrated in its decoder. That
motivates flow-dependent corrections within fixed low-dimensional sensitivity subspaces. A residual memory tracks the
persistent errors that remain, and a variable-wise online gate controls how much of that persistent component is applied.[^aurora]

**Result.** Evaluation uses ERA5 from 1959-1968 for offline initialisation and a strictly chronological 1969-2022 stream
for online evaluation, with updates only from previously available verification targets. Across 69 output fields and five
lead times from 6 to 72 hours, the method reduces MSE and MAE by **6.90% and 6.45%** relative to δ-Adapter, the strongest of
six online adaptation baselines. For 2-metre temperature, larger departures from the historical distribution go with larger
frozen-model errors and larger adaptation gains.[^aurora]

> **Why it matters:** Climate shift is a slow version of the distribution shift in [issue 1](issue-01.html)'s rollout
> discussion. A small online correction on a frozen model is cheaper than periodic retraining, if it holds up outside this setup.

## 6. Release: PhysicsNeMo 26.11 (v2.3.0)

**Source:** NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo release notes for 26.11,[^pnemo] and the v2.3.0 GitHub release, published 21 Nov 2026.[^pnemo-gh]
Performance figures below are **company-reported** from NVIDIA's release notes.

| Area | What the release notes list |
|---|---|
| Stable APIs | GLOBE and the volumetric DiffusionUNet3D leave the experimental namespace. NVIDIA reports GLOBE reaches about 40% lower validation loss on DrivAerML in the same training time. |
| New experimental models | ISLA (mesh-native surrogate whose outputs follow rotations, reflections and translations of the problem); AB-UPT and SMART surface and volume operators (independent implementations of the cited papers); DeFormer (contact-aware crash surrogate). |
| Diffusion | Flow matching, including patch-based multi-diffusion training; new few-step samplers such as DPM-Solver++(2M); regional weather diffusion recipe. |
| Uncertainty quantification | Conformal prediction for spatio-temporal fields; reusable out-of-distribution scorers replace the experimental guardrails; distributed streaming mean and variance so ensemble members and domains can be spread across GPUs. |
| PhysicsNeMo Mesh | Many operations faster with unchanged results; NVIDIA reports vertex angles and curvature up to 1,000x faster on GPU and least-squares gradients 2.4x to 13x faster. |
| Notes | Requires PyTorch 2.13 or newer; removes `physicsnemo.deploy` and the `physicsnemo.compat` layer. |

For this newsletter's themes, the UQ additions stand out: conformal prediction gives distribution-free coverage guarantees
under an exchangeability assumption, in the same spirit as the "guarantees by construction" above.
